Short answer

Use AI as a tutor, not a shortcut. Ask it to explain topics at your level, turn your materials into notes and flashcards, quiz you, plan your revision and check your own answers. Avoid copying answers you don't understand, and follow your school's AI rules.

"How to use AI for studying" and "AI for students" are some of the most searched AI topics, and for good reason. Used well, AI is like a patient tutor available at 2 a.m. Used badly, it's a fast way to learn nothing. Here are seven methods that actually help you learn, each with a prompt you can copy.

1. Get topics explained at your level

Textbooks are written for everyone. AI can explain for you, then go deeper as you ask.

  • "Explain photosynthesis like I'm 15. Then give me one real-life example and one common misconception."

2. Turn your materials into notes

Upload slides, a PDF chapter or a photo of your notes and turn them into something you can revise from. See chat with PDF.

  • "Turn this chapter into one page of revision notes. Bold the key terms and add a 5-line summary at the end."

3. Quiz yourself (active recall)

Testing yourself is one of the best-supported study techniques. Let AI write the questions, and answer them without looking.

  • "Give me 10 exam-style questions on this topic, one at a time. Wait for my answer, then tell me if I'm right and why."

4. Plan your revision

A plan you'll actually follow beats a perfect plan you won't.

  • "I have 7 days until my chemistry exam on these 5 topics. I'm weakest at the second one. Make a daily plan with short sessions and review days."

5. Check your own work

Write your answer first, then ask for feedback. This keeps the thinking yours.

  • "Here's my answer to this question. What did I get right, what's missing, and how would I get full marks?"

6. Get unstuck on problems, step by step

For math, physics or coding, use a reasoning model and ask for the method, not just the answer. You can also snap a picture of a question.

  • "Don't give me the final answer yet. Give me a hint for the first step."

7. Research with real sources

For essays and projects, use AI with web search so every fact has a link you can check and cite. See AI with web search.

  • "Find 5 reliable sources on the causes of World War I, with one line on each and a link."

What not to do

  • Don't copy answers you don't understand. You'll be on your own in the exam.
  • Don't hand in AI-written work as yours. Most schools treat that as cheating.
  • Don't trust every fact. AI makes mistakes; check numbers, dates and quotes.
