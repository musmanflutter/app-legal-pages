Short answer

AI chatbots remember things in two ways: inside one chat (limited by the model's context window) and across chats (only if the app has a memory feature). No chatbot remembers everything forever. The best memory comes from apps that let you control what stays remembered, like pinned messages.

"Which AI chatbot has the best memory?" and "can AI chatbots remember past conversations?" are both common questions, usually asked by someone who just watched an AI forget an instruction they gave twenty messages ago. Here's what's actually happening, and how to fix it.

How AI memory really works

1. Memory inside a chat: the context window

When you send a message, the app sends the model your conversation so far. The model can only read a limited amount of text at once, called its context window. Modern models have large windows, but very long chats, big documents and pasted files fill them up.

When a chat gets long, apps have to trim or summarize older messages to fit. That's when an instruction from the start, like "always answer in bullet points", can quietly disappear.

2. Memory across chats: saved memories

Some AI apps can remember facts about you between separate chats, like your name, job or preferences. ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini each have features of this kind. They're useful, but you don't fully control what gets saved, and they're tied to that one company's app.

Why your AI "forgets"

  • The chat is too long. Early messages fall out of the context window or get trimmed.
  • The instruction was buried. A rule in message 3 competes with hundreds of newer messages.
  • You switched topics. The model follows the most recent direction.
  • You started a new chat. Without a memory feature, a new chat starts from zero.

How to make any AI remember what matters

  1. Put rules in one clear message. "For this whole chat: answer in 3 steps, keep it under 150 words."
  2. Remind it when it drifts. "Remember the 3-step format from the start."
  3. Restart with a summary. Ask for "a summary of everything important so far", then paste it into a new chat.
  4. Reply to the exact message. Quoting the message you mean removes guesswork.
  5. Pin what must never be forgotten. If your app supports pinning, use it for instructions and key facts.

For memory across chats, the big single-company apps each have their own saved-memory features. For memory inside long chats, the winner is whichever app lets you control what stays remembered.
